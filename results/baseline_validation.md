# Baseline training and validation

Run on 2026-09-21 with `configs/baseline_ridge.json` and the frozen Milestone 3 primary table and seed-17 cell-line split. **These are validation results, not final test results.** No test prediction was generated; no model was refit on training plus validation. The supplied `Y` scale and 1,474-row Uprosertib exclusion were preserved. Reproduce with `& ".venv\Scripts\python.exe" "src\run_baselines.py"`, then `& ".venv\Scripts\python.exe" "src\check_baselines.py"` from the repository root. The [alpha search](baseline_alpha_search.csv), [comparison table](baseline_comparison.csv), [per-drug validation table](baseline_validation_by_drug.csv), and [manifest](baseline_validation_manifest.json) provide exact metrics, counts, versions, runtime, and checksums. Fitted models and row-level validation predictions are local and Git-ignored.

## Methods

- Per-drug mean uses training responses only. All 136 primary drugs have training data; training counts range from 33 to 563 (median 523). No drug required a fallback and no drug had a constant training target.
- An additional `StandardScaler` was fitted to the **564 unique training cell lines' saved PCA scores**, once per cell line. It was applied unchanged to the other scores and saved with its output. Fingerprints remain the original binary, unscaled 2,048-bit vectors; `Y` is unchanged.
- Pooled Ridge is additive: `intercept + expression score contribution + fingerprint contribution`. It uses the same expression coefficients for every drug, while fingerprints can provide drug-dependent offsets. For an efficient exact solve, the 136 training-drug fingerprints were represented in an orthonormal basis spanning their row space; this preserves both predictions and the L2 coefficient penalty of Ridge on the original binary fingerprint columns. The saved model has coefficients back in the original 2,048-bit space. The fit used one 264-column training design rather than materializing a 2,176-column response-level matrix for every alpha.
- Per-drug Ridge fits a separate intercept and 128 expression coefficients for each drug. It can learn different expression effects for different known drugs, with less training data per fit. It does not use fingerprints and does not support an unseen-drug claim.
- Both Ridge approaches use positive-alpha [Ridge regression](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.linear_model.Ridge.html) with an unpenalized intercept, solved via centered sufficient statistics and [Cholesky linear solves](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.linalg.cho_solve.html). Sufficient statistics were reused across the seven requested alphas. A synthetic check matched scikit-learn's SVD Ridge predictions after projecting fingerprint coefficients back to the original bits.
- The exact alpha grid was `{0.01, 0.1, 1, 10, 100, 1000, 10000}`. Each approach selected the lowest **overall response-weighted validation RMSE**; per-drug Ridge used **one shared alpha** for all 136 fits. Ties would select the smallest alpha. No alpha was chosen from test data.

Overall metrics pool **63,946 training** or **13,661 validation** response rows, giving each response equal weight. The equal-drug columns are arithmetic means of the **136 separate drug RMSEs or MAEs**, giving each drug equal weight; they are not pooled RMSEs. Spearman is computed within each drug and summarized by the median of defined estimates. The rule requires at least three validation rows, nonconstant targets, and nonconstant predictions. All 136 drugs met the count and target-variation criteria (minimum validation count 5); the mean baseline had constant predictions for every drug, so its 136 correlations are **undefined, not zero**. Both Ridge models had 136 defined correlations. Drug-level counts, errors, correlations, and undefined reasons are recorded in the per-drug table.

Per-drug Ridge lowered response-weighted validation RMSE by **0.1122** relative to pooled Ridge and **0.2916** relative to the mean baseline. Its equal-drug mean RMSE and median within-drug Spearman also improved. This suggests drug-specific expression coefficients help on the frozen validation cell lines for drugs seen in training. The per-drug fit has a larger train-to-validation error gap (0.9886 to 1.2367 RMSE), consistent with greater flexibility; the final test assessment is still pending. The pooled alpha search is nearly flat from 0.01 to 10 (RMSE changes by less than 0.000004), so its numerical selection of 10 should not be overinterpreted. Per-drug Ridge's best grid value was also 10; its gain over alpha 1 was approximately 0.00123 RMSE.

## Verification, runtime, and limits

The baseline script verified Milestone 3 input hashes, cell/drug index and label alignment, training coverage, frozen assignments, and train-only scaling. Six focused synthetic tests pass, including mathematical agreement with reference SVD Ridge and explicit missing Spearman values for constant predictions, insufficient samples, and constant targets. `src/check_baselines.py` independently verifies saved checksums, training means, unique-cell scaler statistics, validation row alignment, and identical validation predictions after model reload. `pip check` found no broken requirements.

The repeat run took **1.40 seconds wall time** on this machine (Python 3.12.8, NumPy 1.26.4, SciPy 1.14.1, scikit-learn 1.9.1; one BLAS thread for Ridge calculations); the first run took 1.53 seconds. The two runs produced identical output-artifact hashes. Timings exclude data retrieval and Milestone 3 preprocessing. Component timings and artifact checksums are in the manifest. BI-2536 has 33 training and 10 validation responses but **no test response** in the frozen split. Some other drugs have few test rows. No split was changed. These validation results concern experimental cell-line response on the supplied `Y` scale and do not establish patient treatment effects or unseen-drug generalization.
